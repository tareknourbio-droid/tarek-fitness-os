What changes as we dive?

Move the depth slider and observe the environment and the illustrative fish model.

👁️ Eyes

At the surface, many fish have eyes suited to brighter conditions. Eye size and structure vary by species.

🫧 Swim bladder (air bladder)

A gas-filled swim bladder can help many bony fish control buoyancy. Gas volume is affected by pressure.

🫁 Gills

Fish use gills to extract dissolved oxygen from water at all depths. Gill design varies with species and lifestyle.

✨ Other adaptations

Sunlight supports photosynthesis in the upper ocean. Deeper down, some animals use sensitive senses or produce their own light.

*Temperature is an illustrative profile, not a universal ocean measurement. Pressure estimate uses about +1 atmosphere per 10 m of seawater (plus 1 atm at the surface). Fish anatomy shown is schematic: deep-sea species are diverse. Some have large light-sensitive eyes, others reduced eyes; many deep-sea fishes have reduced or absent gas swim bladders.
